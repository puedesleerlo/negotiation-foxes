## `faratin1998nego` — Negotiation decision functions for autonomous agents (1998)

- DOI: 10.1016/s0921-8890(98)00029-3
- PDF: downloaded · text in `knowledge/extracted/faratin1998nego.md`

What the Kosmos report attributes to it:

> Here, the reservation value is treated as a parameter of a fitted decision function—typically the Faratin–Sierra–Jennings time-dependent tactic, parameterized by reservation value, deadline, and a concession-shape exponent β ([2.1],

> [2.2]).

> When the opponent uses a Boulware strategy (β < 1), concessions are withheld until near the deadline, providing minimal information for most of the session ([2.1]).

## `baarslag2015lear` — Learning about the opponent in automated bilateral negotiation: a comprehensive survey of opponent modeling techniques (2015)

- DOI: 10.1007/s10458-015-9309-1
- PDF: downloaded · text in `knowledge/extracted/baarslag2015lear.md`

What the Kosmos report attributes to it:

> This approach assumes stationarity in the sense that the opponent's tactic type and parameters do not change mid-session ([3.1], [3.2]).

> The comprehensive survey by Baarslag et al. 2016 catalogues the following accuracy metrics: Euclidean distance on issue weights, mean squared error and mean absolute error on predicted bid utilities, Pearson correlation for preference rankings, and percentage error for reservation-value and deadline estimates ([3.3], [3.4]).

> On the performance side, models are evaluated by negotiation-outcome metrics: average utility, distance to the Pareto frontier, distance to a fair solution (Nash or Kalai–Smorodinsky), agreement percentage, and joint utility ([3.5], [3.6]).

> The survey explicitly notes that accuracy metrics for reservation-value and deadline estimators remain underdeveloped ([3.7]).
