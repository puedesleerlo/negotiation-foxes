## `hendrikx2012eval` — Evaluating the Quality of Opponent Models in Automated Bilateral Negotiations (2012)

- DOI: none
- PDF: downloaded · text in `knowledge/extracted/hendrikx2012eval.md`

What the Kosmos report attributes to it:

> No work was found that estimates the value of perfect information specifically about the counterpart's reservation value, though Hendrikx 2012 benchmarks performance under perfect knowledge of the full preference profile as an upper bound ([11.1], [12.1]).

> Whether opponent modeling improves negotiation outcomes at all is contested: Hendrikx 2012 and Baarslag et al. 2016 document cases where a poor model performs worse than using no model, and the computational cost of modeling may reduce the number of bids explored ([12.2], [12.3]).

## `faratin1998nego` — Negotiation decision functions for autonomous agents (1998)

- DOI: 10.1016/s0921-8890(98)00029-3
- PDF: downloaded · text in `knowledge/extracted/faratin1998nego.md`

What the Kosmos report attributes to it:

> Here, the reservation value is treated as a parameter of a fitted decision function—typically the Faratin–Sierra–Jennings time-dependent tactic, parameterized by reservation value, deadline, and a concession-shape exponent β ([2.1],

> [2.2]).

> When the opponent uses a Boulware strategy (β < 1), concessions are withheld until near the deadline, providing minimal information for most of the session ([2.1]).
